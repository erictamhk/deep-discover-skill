# Initial Plan (SHARED — all three conditions start here)

## Original question
催眠到底是一個真實的意識狀態改變（altered state of consciousness / trance），還是只是社會順從和角色扮演（social compliance / role-playing）？2023-2025 的科學證據到底怎麼說？

## Grounding decision
- Domain: hypnosis research — cognitive neuroscience, clinical psychology, philosophy of mind.
- "狀態改變" (altered state / trance) = the state theory (Hilgard's neodissociation, dissociated-control theory): hypnosis involves a real change in consciousness / cognitive architecture.
- "社會順從" (social compliance / role-playing) = the non-state theory (Spanos, Wagstaff, Kirsch's response set theory): hypnosis is explained by social-cognitive factors, expectations, compliance, without needing a special state.
- "科學證據" = neuroimaging (fMRI, EEG, fNIRS, TMS), behavioral studies, suggestibility scales, meta-analyses.
- The question asks what the evidence actually shows — not what either "side" claims. Establish where evidence is strong, contested, or absent.

## Initial decomposition (sub-questions)
1. What is the state theory and what evidence supports a real altered state (neuroimaging markers, dissociation, automaticity)?
2. What is the non-state theory (social-cognitive, response set, sociocognitive) and what evidence supports it (expectancy effects, context effects, no unique physiological signature)?
3. What do 2023-2025 neuroimaging studies actually find — is there a reliable "hypnosis signature" in the brain, or is it idiosyncratic / heterogeneous?
4. Is there a synthesis position (e.g. Oakley's hybrid, predictive coding accounts) and what does the evidence say about it?
5. What is genuinely unresolved vs established? Where do even the researchers disagree about what the data means?

- Worker 1 (retrieval): sub-question 1 — state theory evidence
- Worker 2 (retrieval): sub-question 2 — non-state theory evidence

- Worker 4 (retrieval): sub-question 4 — synthesis / hybrid / predictive coding accounts

## Verification criteria
Each claim must cite specific evidence. Claims about "what the evidence shows" must distinguish established findings from contested interpretations from genuine gaps. Because this is a two-sided debate, every claim for one side should be checked for what the other side says about the same evidence.
